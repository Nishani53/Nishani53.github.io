The 1.6:1 peg to the Indian Rupee (INR) has been the physical cornerstone of Nepal's economic stability for decades. This agreement, which was established in the 1960s and formalized in 1993, has protected trade across our porous border from volatility. However, a silent revolution is taking place in Birgunj's tea shops and at the checkout counters of Kathmandu's busy markets. 

The "peg" is moving from central bank ledgers to the digital wallets of millions of people as India's Unified Payments Interface (UPI) smoothly combines with Nepal's Fonepay network. As the era of the Digital Peg approaches, our monetary supervision must be drastically rethought to strike a balance between innovation and sovereignty.

## The Death of Distance

Finally, after decades of discussion on the issue, cross-border QR payments between Nepal and India have been launched in June 2023, making the border “frictionless” (eliminating the need for physical currency exchange and reducing the time and costs of transactions). According to Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) data, Indian users have conducted over 1.5 million UPI transactions in Nepal during the last year, which amounts to Rs 4.5 billion. This is because of the growing number of Indian tourists and traders who can use the PhonePe app to pay for goods in Nepal as the conversion happens instantaneously.

This service isn’t just a benefit for tourists; it is a game changer in trade between the two nations. In the first six months of FY 2082/83, the trade deficit with India reached to Rs. 416.71 billion, as imports from India were Rs. 532.45 billion, while exports to India were just Rs. 115.74 billion. Through the standardization of goods movement across borders by making use of QR cross-border payments, it reduces the need for cash as a medium of exchange, which has significantly impacted the trade statistics between the two nations. For small businesses located in Nepal's border towns like Biratnagar, this will allow them to settle their accounts faster and cheaper, while eliminating the need for bargaining over the exchange rate or carrying large amounts of cash.

Indian businesses can use QR payments for their purchases in Nepal, but Nepalese businesses encounter multiple restrictions when they attempt to buy goods from India which includes their e-commerce transactions with Indian representatives. The current situation between the two countries establishes that Nepal must maintain its economic ties with India because full reciprocity between the countries has not yet been achieved. The NRB gains an important opportunity through its digital transformation, but this process brings major dangers to the organization. The NRB will obtain current economic transaction data, which enables the organization to evaluate the complete economic activity in the country; however, the organization will find multiple flaws in its current data gathering system, which was designed for a previous time that used analog methods.

## From Paper Peg to Programmable Peg

The e-Rupee (CBDC-R) program in India reached a significant milestone with Rs 1,016 crore (approximately $122 million) in circulation by March 2025. Following this regional momentum, the Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) is set to launch its own wholesale Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) pilot in 2026. This shift represents evolution rather than mere imitation; in a digital economy, a currency peg can be maintained through code and smart contracts rather than just traditional policy manual interventions.

A "Digital Peg" would enable real-time settlement of trade imbalances, replacing the current system of periodic, manual adjustments. With Nepal’s total trade deficit reaching Rs 797.48 billion in the first half of FY 2082/83, the NRB is currently forced to maintain massive, idle INR reserves to manage the peg. By automating currency exchange at fixed rates via a CBDC platform, Nepal can drastically reduce foreign exchange risks and reallocate these idle reserves toward high-impact domestic goals, such as infrastructure development and green energy projects.

Schemes such as India’s plan to link all BRICS CBDCs to enable faster cross-border settlements can be templates for the whole world to follow. Nepal may need to link up with regional systems such as Bangladesh or Bhutan to create a digital corridor for South Asia. A scenario where Nepali exporters can instantly settle hydropower exports to India without middlemen charging transaction fees can be created. Reducing transaction frictions, estimated to be between 1% and 2% of the trade value, will stabilize the peg and boost economic growth rates in Nepal.

## The Inflation Sentinel

Digital economics could really promote a more transparent border. Analysts estimate that around 20-30% of Nepalese trade with India occurs in a "shadow economy" since most cash transactions at the border can't be tracked and therefore make it difficult to track inflation data. Digital payments reveal this "dark money" and track it as it crosses the border. 

By using information about cross-border QR transactions, the NRB will be able to have a "high-frequency" understanding of how much demand exists for consumers and how much prices are changing. For example, a spike in the amount of QR payments for petroleum products from India may be an indicator that fuel prices will soon rise; in this case, the NRB may take pre-emptive measures to control inflation through monetary policy. Digital balances may therefore serve as an early warning signal for inflationary pressures, particularly important when considering our graduation from LDC status by 2026 during continued global economic uncertainty.

The implementation of UPI in India is a clear example of how these systems increase economic intelligence. In fact, within December of the financial year 2025-26, the UPI transactions reached Rs 230 lakh crore, equivalent to $2.56 trillion in value. This information was used by the Reserve Bank of India to make necessary changes to their economic policy. In our case, we can achieve this by utilizing AI technology to predict and consequently reduce our imported inflation rate, which contributes to our 5-7% annual rate.

## The Sovereign Challenge

Caution should be exercised in integrating with India’s digital infrastructure further. This would create a “digital sovereignty” situation, whereby most transactions would be conducted on rails in Mumbai or Bangalore. In a world dominated by QR codes, the speed of money would increase, leading to a greater boom-and-bust cycle. The tool for reserve requirement would not be effective in a hyper-linked digital world. A surprise inflow of digital money could cause a bubble in property prices and other assets.

There could be a threat from a cyber-security threat as well. If the UPI-Fonepay link were to be breached, millions could lose their money, leading to a lack of trust in the monetary system. Despite good mobile connectivity, meaningful internet access for secure digital transactions remains an obstacle for nearly 40% of the population. This makes it vulnerable to outside forces. The current geopolitical situation due to BRICS digital connections could make the peg a tool for outside forces instead of a stabilizing force.

## Policy Imperatives

Nepal would need to act decisively in the implementation of the Digital Peg, without the ability to compromise on doing so. 

1. **Expedite the NRB CBDC Roadmap:** The roadmap being developed by the NRB must be prioritized. Along with necessary privacy protections, the CBDC must be fully independent and interoperable.
2. **Launch Pilot Programs:** The pilot program, modeled after the expansion of the e-Rupiah in India, should launch wholesale cross-border settlements in 2026.
3. **National Digital ID Integration:** Establish a secure National Digital ID System to ensure safe payment systems, legalization, and regulation of digital assets.
4. **Build Custom Applications:** Work with local technology firms to build customized payment applications to minimize reliance on foreign application platforms.
5. **Bilateral Reciprocity:** Leverage diplomatic channels to advocate for reciprocity in cross-border payments so Nepalis enjoy the same access as Indians to payment networks through a bilateral digital trade agreement.
6. **Institutional Capacity:** Build capacity by training NRB employees in AI and blockchain while collaborating with international organizations like the IMF to share best practices.

## Conclusion

To keep the peg as we transition to a digital economy poses new management challenges rather than continued debate over whether the peg should be maintained. Nepal’s adoption of CBDC and legalizing digital assets are positive steps forward, yet we must not simply adopt technology from elsewhere; we must build a sustainable national digital ecosystem that could connect with the world without being swallowed up. 

A country's economy will never truly thrive within a digital economy without that legacy of sustained close community connections to its citizens; thus, our policies must reflect that.
