## Abstract
This paper analyzes the economic viability and broader market impacts of converting small-diameter timber—harvested during forest fuel reduction and ecological restoration treatments—into mass timber products in eastern Oregon. By integrating supply chain dynamics with economic impact modeling, the study assesses local job creation, value-added contributions, and market opportunities for low-value forest biomass.

## Key Findings
* **Restoration Synergy:** Demonstrates how utilizing small-diameter timber from fuel reduction projects can offset forest management costs while driving regional wood products manufacturing.
* **Economic Development:** Identifies key economic indicators, including regional job creation, labor income, and industrial output generated by potential mass timber processing infrastructure.
* **Biomass Utilization:** Highlights sustainable supply-chain pathways to convert low-value restoration harvest material into high-value engineered mass timber products.
