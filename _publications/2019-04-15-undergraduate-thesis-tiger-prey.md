This is my undergraduate thesis completed at the Institute of Forestry, Pokhara Campus, focusing on the spatial ecology of large mammalian prey species within critical conservation corridors and protected source sites in Nepal.

## Abstract

Large carnivore conservation, including tiger (*Panthera tigris*) recovery, depends fundamentally on maintaining healthy, resilient prey populations. This study systematically assesses how human disturbance gradients (livestock grazing, human encounters), micro-habitat types (grasslands, mixed forest, riverine forest, agricultural edges), and management regimes affect the distribution and Relative Abundance Index (RAI) of key ungulate prey species across Bardia National Park and the vital adjoining Khata Corridor.

### Core Dimensions of the Study:
* **Disturbance Response Modeling:** Evaluating how different prey types adapt to or are displaced by human footprint variables, demonstrating varied tolerances among specialized grazers versus generalist browsers.
* **Habitat Mosaic Analysis:** Quantifying the critical baseline value of structural mixed forest and tall grassland ecotones (transition zones) in supporting high-density prey assemblages.
* **Landscape Connectivity evaluation:** Analyzing the relative effectiveness of community-managed corridor buffers compared to strictly enforced National Park boundaries in securing contiguous, functional foraging pathways.
