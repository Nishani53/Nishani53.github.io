## Summary

This paper evaluates the effectiveness of different management regimes—National Parks, Buffer Zones, and unprotected Corridors/Forests—in safeguarding wildlife and mitigating the impacts of structural habitat loss. Using extensive camera-trap data, the authors track the occupancy dynamics of 17 medium and large mammal species across the sub-Himalayan lowlands of Nepal.

### Core Dimensions of the Study:
* **Multi-Species Occupancy Modeling:** Evaluates how community-managed areas and corridors perform compared to strictly protected National Parks for diverse mammalian communities.
* **Habitat Loss & Management Buffers:** Assesses how localized habitat degradation impacts species distribution and checks if community-led buffers successfully absorb edge-effect pressures.
* **Conservation Insights:** Highlights the critical role of functional wildlife corridors outside of core park boundaries to sustain long-term regional meta-populations.
