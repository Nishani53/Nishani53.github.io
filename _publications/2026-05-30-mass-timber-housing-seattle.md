This opinion article explores how mass timber construction offers a faster, lower-carbon alternative for multifamily housing in Seattle while simultaneously creating jobs in rural timber communities and funding vital wildfire risk reduction efforts.

## Summary

The piece examines the bottlenecks slowing the local adoption of cross-laminated timber (CLT) despite progressive state building codes, highlighting the structural, environmental, and economic advantages of utilizing smaller-diameter wood from forest-thinning operations.

### Key Discussion Points:
* **Construction Efficiency:** Mass timber structures assemble roughly 25% faster than concrete equivalents, drastically reducing on-site delays and structural timelines.
* **Carbon and Climate Impact:** Substituting traditional concrete and steel with engineered wood offers immense life-cycle carbon storage advantages, trapping CO₂ within the building frame.
* **Fire Performance Realities:** The charring mechanics of thick CLT panels insulate the structural core, outperforming unprotected steel under extreme thermal loads.
* **Rural-Urban Economic Synergy:** Sourcing CLT from small-diameter timber connects urban housing demands with restoration forestry and mill jobs in rural communities.
* **Regulatory Hurdles:** Addressing the municipal permitting and review bottlenecks that keep developers default-aligned with traditional concrete construction.
