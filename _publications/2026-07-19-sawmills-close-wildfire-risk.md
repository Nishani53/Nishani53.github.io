This opinion article examines the critical bottleneck in wildfire mitigation caused by the structural collapse of local timber infrastructure, highlighting how mill closures directly leave forest restoration projects stalled and communities vulnerable.

## Summary

The piece discusses the disconnect between public funding for forest management and the disappearance of the independent regional mills required to process low-value, small-diameter logs, arguing that wood-processing capacity must be treated as a vital public utility.

### Key Discussion Points:
* **The "Dead End" of Thinning:** Even when forestry technicians mark small-diameter trees for hazardous fuel removal, projects draw zero bids because nearby mill closures make hauling logistics financially impossible.
* **Infrastructure Collapse:** Highlighting the permanent closure of seven Oregon lumber mills in 2024 alone, which eliminated hundreds of rural jobs and severely shrunk the sector's operational capacity.
* **The Unforgiving Math:** Mechanical thinning and hauling costs between $1,200 and $2,000 per acre, meaning forest restoration cannot survive without local processing gates open to absorb the material.
* **Policy Funding Mismatch:** Addressing how multi-billion-dollar federal investments fund the tools for cutting (the chainsaw) but completely overlook the destination for the logs (the mill).
* **A Public Utility Reframe:** Advocating for state and federal policymakers to stabilize independent mills using public utility funds and to tie wildfire-reduction grants directly to infrastructure preservation.
