This is my Master of Science thesis completed at Michigan State University, focusing on the intersection of forest products optimization, supply chain logistics, and regional macroeconomics.

## Abstract

Mass timber technologies, such as cross-laminated timber (CLT), present a significant opportunity to link sustainable forest management with low-carbon building construction. This research provides a comprehensive assessment of sustainable wood feedstock availability across Michigan's distinct forest zones, identifies optimal geographic cluster points for prospective manufacturing facilities, and estimates the localized macroeconomic impacts.

### Core Dimensions of the Study:
* **Feedstock Resource Assessment:** Quantifying sustainable harvest volumes and species compositions across regional procurement zones to ensure long-term supply chain stability.
* **Spatial Optimization Modeling:** Utilizing network analysis and location-allocation frameworks to determine optimal facility locations relative to forest resources and major structural demand hubs.
* **Economic Impact Analysis:** Leveraging regional input-output models to quantify the direct, indirect, and induced annual effects on employment, labor income, and Total Value Added.
